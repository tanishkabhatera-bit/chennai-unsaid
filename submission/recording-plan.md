## Part 1 · Face clips (phone, front camera)

**Setup:** daylight from a window in front of you, phone at eye level, plain wall behind. Talk like you're telling a friend, not reading. Record each clip 3 times and keep the best.

**FACE 1 · the story (0:00, about 16 seconds)**

> "I fainted from the heat in Chennai once.
> Nobody had told me how hot it actually gets.
>
> And when you're looking for a place to live, it's the same.
> The listing just says 'very safe area'.
> Nobody tells you if the street floods.
> You find out after you've moved in."

Small pause after "how hot it actually gets". Say "very safe area" with a little smile, like you don't believe it. Stop after "moved in" and hold your face for half a second.

**FACE 2 · the turn (0:19, about 8 seconds)**

> "So I built Chennai Unsaid.
> Now, before you move in, you can see how high the water came, how hot it gets, and where to find shade and water nearby."

Say it with a small smile. This is where the website starts.

**FACE 3 · the close (2:33, about 17 seconds)**

> "Before this, you found out after you moved in.
> Now you can check before you sign.
> And when the heat hits, you know where the nearest shade and water are.
>
> So check the area.
> And if you know a shady spot, add it.
> Someone will need it next summer."

After the last word, keep looking at the camera for 1 second.

## Part 2 · Voiceover (phone voice recorder)

Record each as its own file: VO1 to VO5. Speak a little slower than normal, with a 1-second pause at the start and end.

**VO1 · flood history (about 18 seconds)**

> "Type any area. I'll take Velachery.
> Tap 2023, and the water climbs to where the news said it reached. Chest deep, Cyclone Michaung.
> And right below it, the article it came from.
> Ten years of floods for 202 areas, in one place, every mark with a source."

**VO2 · heat (about 6 seconds)**

> "Flip to heat. Today's temperature, live, and the worst day of the past year."

**VO3 · check a property (about 20 seconds)**

> "Now the part brokers won't like.
> Paste their message. It reads every claim.
> 'No flooding problem in this street.' The record says five flood years.
> So instead of finding out in November, you ask the right questions before you pay the advance."

**VO4 · Residents' Map (about 42 seconds)**

> "Knowing the problem is one thing. But when it's 40 degrees outside, you need somewhere to go.
> That's why I added the Residents' Map.
> Where's the shade. Where's free drinking water. Which roads have none. Which streets are under water.
> Not tips. Actual places, with a photo and a date.
> This one's from a resident: the Metro Water tanker in Vyasarpadi.
> Anyone can add a spot, and every photo is checked before it shows up.
> So the next person stuck in the heat knows where the nearest shade and water are.
> The goal is 100 spots before next summer."

**VO5 · AWS (about 32 seconds)**

> "Here's what runs it.
> Amazon Bedrock read 190 Chennai news articles and turned them into flood records, then checked every record a second time.
> DynamoDB stores those records, the heat data, residents' reports and the map.
> Photos sit in a private S3 bucket, with location data stripped out.
> And the whole site runs on AWS Amplify, with no keys in the code."
